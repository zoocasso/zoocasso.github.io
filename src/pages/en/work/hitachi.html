Context

The project produces AI training data from images taken with industrial machine-vision cameras. The code I inherited in April 2025 could not be maintained. At first I fixed only what broke, but every change in capture conditions broke something else, and every dataset needed a developer sitting next to it.

I concluded the problem was the structure, not the symptoms: creating a dataset was tied to one developer.

My part

What I did

  • Rewrote the pipeline in Python, from camera control and capture to preprocessing model-ready datasets
  • The measurement and analysis program
  • A PyQt GUI for non-developer researchers, shipped as a PyInstaller executable
  • Handover to two juniors, on-site deployment and the demo

What others did

  • Building the hardware in the lab on site: a junior researcher
  • Preparing samples and reference labels: the Dutch researchers

Decisions

  1. Stop patching and rewrite the whole thing

    Why
    Each fix broke something else, and every patch tied dataset creation more tightly to a developer.
    How
    I rewrote everything from camera control and capture to dataset preprocessing as one Python pipeline.

  2. Replace it step by step against the old results

    Why
    A full rewrite risked changing the results without anyone noticing.
    How
    On the same inputs, I checked that the new code's numbers matched the old code's before replacing each stage.

  3. A GUI that non-developers run themselves

    Why
    Researchers are the ones who need datasets, but only a developer could run the code.
    How
    I built a PyQt GUI and shipped it as a PyInstaller executable, so researchers create datasets without a development environment.

  4. Hand over the design intent before the code

    Why
    The project had to keep going without me.
    How
    I walked two juniors through why each stage is split the way it is; one of them built the hardware on site.

On site in the Netherlands (Sep–Oct 2026)

Our lab and Hitachi staff were given a lab at a research institute in the Netherlands. A junior researcher built the hardware, the Dutch researchers prepared the samples and the reference labels, and my measurement and analysis program turned the captures into AI training data.

Hitachi staff used the program themselves on site, and I later demonstrated it to the Dutch researchers. Both the people doing the work and the project partner rated it well. The work on site was in Japanese and English.
